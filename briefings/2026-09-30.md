# UK Higher Education Briefing

**Wednesday, 30 September 2026**

Today has two genuinely major developments. The first is a significant escalation in research-security policy, with MI5 directly warning universities about a Chinese organisation. The second is a milestone for UK international education: **TNE has overtaken international students studying in the UK for the first time**.

## 1. MI5 tells UK universities to cut links with Chinese research body

MI5 has issued an unusual public espionage alert concerning the China General Technology Research Institute (CGTRI), which it says has been used by China's Ministry of State Security to obtain sensitive UK research. More than **100 UK-linked academics** are reported to have received funding connected with the organisation, including research involving AI, cybersecurity, covert communications and steganography. Security minister Dan Jarvis has written to university leaders asking institutions to end relevant collaborations; MI5 also warns that future cooperation could engage the National Security Act 2023. **Why it matters:** This is materially different from generic government warnings about research security. Universities now have a named counterparty and an explicit instruction to review or terminate relationships, making **counterparty identification, beneficial ownership, funding provenance and research-security due diligence** operational requirements rather than abstract compliance concerns.

It also sharpens the strategic problem identified in last week's Manchester research-security evidence: the UK wants extensive collaboration with China while simultaneously restricting collaboration in sensitive technologies. Universities need risk-based screening capable of distinguishing ordinary academic cooperation from genuinely sensitive relationships, rather than simply treating "China" as the risk variable.

## 2. UK TNE passes 699,000 students and overtakes onshore international education

New Universities UK International data show **699,660 students studying for UK qualifications overseas in 2024/25**, up 7% in one year and **36.6% over five years**. That exceeds the **681,430 international students studying in the UK**, marking the first time UK TNE provision has become larger than the UK's onshore international student population. Some 183 UK providers now deliver TNE across 229 countries and territories. ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/uk-tne-students-near-700k-as-overseas-provision-overtakes-onshore-international-enrolment/ "UK TNE students near 700k as overseas provision overtakes onshore international enrolment"))

The composition is even more interesting. **Partnership provision has grown 51.4% in five years**, compared with 30.3% for overseas campuses and only 6.8% for distance learning. South Asia has grown particularly rapidly, reaching 124,500 students, **76.9% above 2020/21**. ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/uk-tne-students-near-700k-as-overseas-provision-overtakes-onshore-international-enrolment/ "UK TNE students near 700k as overseas provision overtakes onshore international enrolment"))

**Why it matters:** This is more than another record TNE number. The centre of gravity of UK international education is changing. TNE can no longer sensibly be treated as a peripheral international-office activity alongside the "real" business of recruiting international students to Britain.

The partnership growth figure is particularly significant. The dominant scalable model appears increasingly to be **institution-to-institution educational partnerships**, rather than expensive wholly owned branch campuses. That places partner selection, academic governance, quality assurance, relationship management and scalable operating systems much closer to the centre of university strategy.

## 5. Government's regional HE agenda is moving towards genuine course-level collaboration

New analysis today of the government's post-16 and regional skills agenda highlights an increasingly consequential policy direction: universities and FE providers are being encouraged to coordinate provision around **regional economic priorities rather than independently maintaining overlapping portfolios**. Skills minister Jacqui Smith recently reiterated that universities, colleges, employers and local leaders should jointly identify regional skills needs; the CMA has meanwhile issued guidance on HE collaboration and the OfS has developed a prototype market-monitoring tool. ([Wonkhe](https://wonkhe.com/blogs/higher-education-can-become-more-collaborative-in-course-provision-but-the-onus-is-on-institutions-to-work-out-how "Higher education can become more collaborative in course provision – but the onus is on institutions to work out how – Wonkhe"))

**Why it matters:** This could eventually go considerably further than friendly regional alliances. Meaningful collaboration might involve institutions deciding **who teaches what**, jointly investing in expensive provision, constructing FE-to-HE pathways, sharing infrastructure, or deliberately concentrating particular subjects in particular institutions.

That creates difficult questions around institutional autonomy and competition law, but financial pressure makes the economics increasingly compelling. The LLE could reinforce the model by making modular progression between providers easier. ([Wonkhe](https://wonkhe.com/blogs/higher-education-can-become-more-collaborative-in-course-provision-but-the-onus-is-on-institutions-to-work-out-how "Higher education can become more collaborative in course provision – but the onus is on institutions to work out how – Wonkhe"))

### Strategic read

**The TNE numbers are today's most important development for international strategy, even though the MI5 story is the bigger immediate news story.**

The crucial statistic is not simply **699,660**. It is this:

**Partnership TNE: +51.4% in five years.** ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/uk-tne-students-near-700k-as-overseas-provision-overtakes-onshore-international-enrolment/ "UK TNE students near 700k as overseas provision overtakes onshore international enrolment"))

That tells us something about the business model. International branch campuses attract enormous attention because they are visible, but the faster-scaling mechanism is partnership provision.

There is also an interesting geographical signal. South Asian TNE has expanded **76.9% since 2020/21**. ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/uk-tne-students-near-700k-as-overseas-provision-overtakes-onshore-international-enrolment/ "UK TNE students near 700k as overseas provision overtakes onshore international enrolment")) That should make India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and neighbouring markets particularly important when universities construct their partnership pipelines.

The combination of today's first two stories creates a strategic tension worth paying attention to:

**UK universities are becoming substantially more internationally embedded at precisely the point when government expects substantially stronger institutional control over international relationships.**

That means international partnership growth cannot simply be measured by number of agreements, students or revenue. Universities increasingly need a **portfolio-management capability** that simultaneously assesses commercial potential, academic quality, regulatory exposure, geopolitical risk, research security, partner dependence and concentration risk.

For a university trying to expand TNE substantially, that capability may become as important as the ability to find new partners in the first place.
