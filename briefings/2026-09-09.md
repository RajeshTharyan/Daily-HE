# UK Higher Education Daily Briefing

9 September 2026 | Five developments ranked by strategic importance

## 1. DfE launches a national community-cohesion framework for universities


- Development: The Department for Education, University of Salford and NUS have launched work on a national
framework and toolkit for community cohesion across universities, colleges and student unions.

- What it will do: The framework will set principles for student conduct and engagement around divisive issues, with
empirical research, focus groups and stakeholder interviews feeding into a first draft by year-end.

- Why it matters: This adds another governance layer to universities already balancing free speech, Prevent,
harassment duties and campus tensions.

- Strategic implication: Institutions should align cohesion initiatives with existing free-speech, safeguarding and
student-conduct processes rather than create a parallel compliance bureaucracy.

- Watch next: Whether the framework remains voluntary guidance or begins to shape regulatory expectations and
institutional risk assessment.

Source: Department for Education, 9 September 2026.


## 2. Greek and Scottish universities deepen structured partnership-building


- Development: Greek and Scottish universities held a matchmaking event at EAIE 2026 under the UK-Greece
Strategic Partnership in Education.

- Scale: The programme now involves 24 Greek universities, more than 50 UK institutions and over 500 academics
and staff, indicating a maturing bilateral partnership platform.

- Why it matters: It demonstrates a shift from isolated MoUs towards government- and sector-supported pipelines for
joint education, mobility and academic collaboration.

- Strategic implication: Universities should treat such national frameworks as lower-cost routes to partner discovery
and portfolio development, but prioritise agreements with defined academic and commercial outputs.

- Watch next: Concrete dual degrees, TNE programmes, research projects and mobility agreements emerging from
the matchmaking process.

Source: Study in Greece / British Council, 9 September 2026.


## 3. New evidence highlights a large gender gap in the domestic university pipeline


- Development: HEPI highlights UCAS data showing 33,000 fewer UK-domiciled 18-year-old boys accepted to
university than girls in the latest cycle.

- Evidence: The commentary argues that persistent attainment gaps at school feed directly into lower male
participation in higher education.

- Why it matters: At a time of recruitment pressure, the participation gap represents both an access problem and a
potentially material pool of unrealised domestic demand.

- Strategic implication: Universities should examine subject-level and regional male participation patterns and work
with schools on targeted outreach rather than treating widening participation mainly through conventional
socioeconomic metrics.

- Watch next: Whether government or OfS access policy gives greater weight to sex-based participation gaps.

Source: HEPI, 9 September 2026, drawing on UCAS data.


## 4. Imperial-IIT Bombay partnership shows the direction of UK-India collaboration


- Development: Imperial College London and IIT Bombay have launched a strategic partnership spanning research,
innovation and education, initially focused on clean technology and medical technology.

- Model: Planned activity includes joint research and innovation programmes, academic workshops and student
entrepreneurship initiatives, building on an established research relationship.

- Why it matters: India is increasingly a location for deep institutional collaboration rather than simply a source market
for UK international recruitment.

- Strategic implication: UK institutions need India strategies that integrate research, education, entrepreneurship and
local networks; recruitment-only models will look increasingly thin.

- Watch next: Joint funding, new education pathways and whether the partnership develops into more formal TNE or
physical presence.

Source: Imperial College London, 7 September 2026.


## 5. £10m international TB programme reinforces the value of research consortia


- Development: Queen Mary University of London has joined RESPECT-TB, a £10 million international programme on
drug-resistant tuberculosis involving 15 organisations across five high-burden countries.

- Model: Queen Mary is coordinating with Oxford, the University of the Witwatersrand and national TB programmes to
build a major cross-country evidence resource.

- Why it matters: Large international consortia remain an important route to research funding, global impact and
durable institutional relationships at a time of pressure on university finances.

- Strategic implication: Universities should build partnership capability around thematic research platforms that can
combine grant income, data assets, policy engagement and long-term international networks.

- Watch next: Follow-on funding, shared datasets and whether the programme generates wider institutional
partnerships in participating countries.

Source: Queen Mary University of London, 8 September 2026.
