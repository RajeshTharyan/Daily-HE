## 1. UCL changes assessment platforms over future student-data concerns
Published 16 November 2026

### What happened

UCL is suspending Turnitin Feedback Studio for submission and marking, moving those functions to Moodle Assignment or Moodle Coursework over the academic year. UCL says proposed licence changes would have allowed student submissions to train AI systems. Turnitin has deferred those changes for 2026/27, but uncertainty beyond 1 September 2027 informed UCL’s decision. Similarity checking will continue. UCL states that it does not use Turnitin’s AI-writing detection and that work submitted for similarity checking is not used to train generative AI models.

### Why it matters

The decision shows how changes to supplier terms can affect assessment delivery even before those terms take effect. It also makes the distinction between submission, marking, similarity checking and AI detection operationally important.

### Strategic implications and what to watch (analysis)

Universities should map which assessment functions depend on each supplier and establish how a platform change would affect feedback, moderation and access to historical work. For international partnerships, equivalent processes and clear student communications would need to extend across delivery locations. Watch for revised supplier terms and UCL’s implementation experience; this announcement does not establish that other institutions must adopt the same response.

## 2. Home Office rejects a covert student cap while emphasising sponsor compliance
Conference and reporting: 16 November 2026

### What happened

The PIE News reports that Home Office official Oliver Rae told the UUK International recruitment conference that tighter compliance requirements and visa refusals are not a blanket policy to control international student numbers. Separately, Times Higher Education reports that he urged universities to share recruitment, enrolment and completion information proactively and improve communication between internal teams.

### Verified policy context

A Home Office parliamentary answer dated 16 November states that Basic Compliance Assessment requirements are a visa-refusal rate below 5%, enrolment of at least 95% and course completion of at least 85%. It states that the completion threshold rises to at least 90% from 1 June 2027.

### Why it matters and strategic implications (analysis)

Recruitment growth depends on the institution’s capacity to manage compliance throughout the student lifecycle. Admissions, international recruitment, academic departments and student services therefore need a shared view of emerging risks. For business schools, recruitment scenarios should consider enrolment and completion outcomes alongside offer conversion. The conference remarks do not constitute a new policy announcement.

### What to watch (analysis)

Monitor formal sponsor guidance and whether communication with UKVI becomes more timely and actionable. Institutional planning should use the official definitions and assessment periods, rather than treating headline percentages as a complete compliance model.

## 3. OfS evidence strengthens the case for programme-level assessment reform
Published 11 November 2026; continuing strategic issue

### What happened

An OfS and Advance HE report draws on conversations with more than 300 students and staff from over 120 institutions. It identifies risks to assessment validity where students can use AI to complete unsupervised work without demonstrating the intended knowledge and skills. It also notes limitations in AI detection, inconsistent understanding of permitted use and uneven provision of AI-literacy training.

### Why it matters

The report supports robust assessment that establishes individual achievement alongside carefully designed activities that integrate AI. It recognises potential learning benefits while warning that outsourcing demanding intellectual tasks can undermine learning. This is relevant to both assurance of awards and graduate capability.

### Strategic implications and what to watch (analysis)

A useful programme review would identify which outcomes require direct demonstration of independent knowledge and which involve competent, critical use of AI. Business schools could combine selected supervised assessments with applied work requiring students to justify assumptions, evaluate outputs and defend decisions. Any redesign should account for accessibility, staff workload and consistency across partner delivery. Watch for further regulatory clarification and practical evidence on whether revised assessments actually distinguish student understanding from the quality of an AI-generated submission.

### Sources

OfS and Advance HE report and executive summary: https://www.officeforstudents.org.uk/publications/making-ai-work-for-students-reflections-on-institutional-practice/

## 4. Surrey explores a specialist UK–Indonesia dual master’s partnership
Published 13 November 2026; retained for TNE relevance

### What happened

Surrey Business School has secured a British Council TNE Exploratory Grant for work with Universitas Padjadjaran in Indonesia. The Space Futures project will explore a dual-degree master’s specialising in the space economy. Work is scheduled to begin in January 2027, examining educational and industry needs and developing the proposed curriculum. Planned collaboration includes staff exchanges, shared dissertation supervision, student projects and a community connecting academics, businesses and policymakers.

### Why it matters

This offers a concrete example of a business school developing international provision around a specialist industry and wider institutional expertise. The announcement concerns exploratory development; it does not confirm an approved programme, student intake or commercial viability.

### Strategic implications and what to watch (analysis)

For partnership strategy, the useful question is whether a distinctive academic capability matches identifiable employer needs and student demand in the partner market. Exploratory funding can support that assessment, but a viable launch still requires a credible delivery and financial model. Watch for evidence on demand, approvals, responsibilities for teaching and supervision, pricing and the proposed five-year collaboration roadmap. The model may inform partnership development elsewhere, provided market assumptions are tested locally.

### Sources

University of Surrey announcement: https://www.surrey.ac.uk/news/funding-secured-uk-indonesia-partnership-develop-future-space-economy-leaders

## 5. OfS interim financial return: a monitoring milestone, not a new announcement
Request updated 3 September 2026; submission deadline 13 November 2026

### Correction and verified position

The earlier email described this too loosely as a November update. The OfS published the November interim-return requirements in September, with submissions due on 13 November. Its page identifies this as a single-year return, separate from the Annual Financial Return issued in April. The request includes a notice under ongoing condition F3 and guidance on the information providers must supply.

### Why it matters

The return supports the regulator’s financial monitoring. Its existence alone does not establish that a particular university is in difficulty or that sector finances deteriorated during November. It should therefore be treated as a continuing governance issue rather than fresh news from the last 24 hours.

### Strategic implications and what to watch (analysis)

For university leaders, the practical issue is whether the financial information supplied to the regulator is consistent with internal forecasts and decisions on staffing, recruitment and investment. International partnerships and new programmes should be assessed against realistic cash-flow timing and delivery costs. Watch for subsequent OfS publications or institutional follow-up arising from this reporting cycle; no new findings from these returns are asserted here.

OfS interim financial return: deadlines and requirements: https://www.officeforstudents.org.uk/publications/interim-financial-return-deadlines-and-requirements/
